## Total

**8 HIT, 7 MISS** out of 15 scored claims; 3 honest "no prediction" answers.

Where we were right: colour and Maillard browning, the gross effect of diluting starch,
losses of heat-sensitive compounds. These follow from simple chemistry.

Where we were wrong: every prediction about how **milk protein interacts with starch
and water**. That covers:
- WSI, WAI and RVA with NDM, all three in the opposite direction;
- the effect of acid on browning;
- small WPC additions, which improved expansion and lowered density instead of the
  opposite.

The paper's own explanation for RVA is that protein competes for water, so less starch
gelatinises in the barrel. Our "dilution" reasoning missed that.

Consequence for the chrupka (WPI 90, about 87 % protein): the part of our reasoning that
failed is exactly the part that matters for a high-protein product. Reasoning from
general principles is not good enough there. It needs data from our own line.
